Project Abstract
Oral Squamous Cell Carcinoma (OSCC) is among the most prevalent forms of head and
neck cancer worldwide and is the leading cancer diagnosed in Pakistani males and the
second most common among females, driven largely by widespread use of tobacco, betel
quid, and areca nut. Delayed diagnosis, caused by limited access to specialist oncologists
and the time-consuming, subjective nature of manual histopathological examination,
remains the principal cause of poor prognosis in this region. Although several studies
have applied Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) to oral cancer histopathology, a
systematic, head-to-head comparison of CNN-based and Vision Transformer (ViT)-based
architectures on OSCC-specific datasets, combined end-to-end with a patient-facing
support system, is still largely missing from the literature; this constitutes the research
gap addressed by this project. This Final Year Project proposes Health Intelligence, a
web-based system that (i) classifies Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E)-stained histopathology
images as OSCC or nonOSCC using both CNN architectures (ResNet, EfficientNet,
DenseNet) and a Vision Transformer, benchmarking them using accuracy, precision, recall,
F1-score, and AUCROC; (ii) recommends the nearest oncology and ENT hospitals to a
flagged patient using a curated database of Pakistani oncology facilities and a
geolocation-based nearest-facility algorithm; and (iii) provides an AI chatbot that supports
patients with medication reminders, dietary and lifestyle precautions, and moral support.
Publicly available histopathology datasets, including repositories hosted on Zenodo,
GitHub, and Kaggle, will be used for model development, training, and evaluation. The
expected contribution of this work is twofold: a validated comparative study of CNN
versus ViT performance on OSCC classification, and a deployable, Pakistan-contextualized
decision-support web application that is, to the best of our knowledge, among the first to
combine OSCC detection, geolocation-based hospital recommendation, and
conversational patient support within a single integrated system.

1. Introduction

Digital pathology and medical image analysis form a rapidly growing research domain in
which artificial intelligence (AI) is used to assist pathologists in examining tissue samples
for signs of disease. Within this domain, the automated classification of histopathological
images for cancer detection has emerged as one of the most active and clinically relevant
applications of deep learning. This project situates itself in the intersection of computer
vision, deep learning, and oncology-focused health informatics, specifically targeting Oral
Squamous Cell Carcinoma (OSCC), the most common malignant tumor of the oral cavity.
This research domain is important because OSCC accounts for over ninety percent of all
oral cancers and continues to be diagnosed late in most developing countries, including
Pakistan, where oral cancer is the most frequently diagnosed cancer among men and the
second most common among women. Histopathological examination of biopsy tissue
remains the diagnostic gold standard, but it is invasive, time-consuming, and subject to
inter-observer variability among pathologists, particularly in a country such as Pakistan
where there is an acute shortage of trained oral pathologists relative to the number of
at-risk patients.
The current situation is characterized by two related deficiencies. First, deep learning
research on OSCC histopathology has so far concentrated almost exclusively on CNN
architectures, with comparatively little systematic benchmarking against modern Vision
Transformer (ViT) architectures that can model long-range dependencies across a tissue
image rather than relying purely on local convolutional receptive fields. Second, even


                                             4
where accurate detection models exist, there is no readily available system that connects
a positive or suspicious OSCC screening result to an actionable next step for a patient in
Pakistan, such as identifying a nearby oncology facility or providing ongoing support
during treatment.
Computational and AI-driven research is directly relevant here because it can standardize
and accelerate the interpretation of histopathological images, reduce the diagnostic
burden on a limited pool of specialists, and, when combined with geolocation data and
conversational AI, extend the benefits of early detection into practical, patient-centered
support. This project therefore proposes an integrated, three-module web application,
Health Intelligence, that performs OSCC detection from histopathology images using both
CNN and ViT architectures, recommends nearby oncology hospitals based on the
patient's location, and offers an AI chatbot for medication reminders, precautionary
guidance, and moral support.


2. Problem Statement
Existing deep learning research on Oral Squamous Cell Carcinoma (OSCC) histopathology
has predominantly evaluated CNN-based architectures in isolation, with only limited and
inconsistent comparison against Vision Transformer (ViT)-based architectures on
OSCCspecific datasets. This leaves a methodological gap: it is not clearly established,
under a common experimental protocol and a consistent set of evaluation metrics,
whether ViTbased models offer a meaningful advantage over CNN-based models for
OSCC histopathology classification, or whether the additional computational cost of
transformerbased architectures is justified for this particular task.
A second, complementary gap exists at the systems level. Even where an OSCC
classification model performs well, there is no publicly documented,
Pakistancontextualized system that closes the loop between a screening result and
patient action: guiding a patient (or the healthcare worker assisting them) toward the
nearest appropriate oncology facility, and supporting the patient with continued
medication adherence, dietary precautions, and moral support during an emotionally
difficult period. The absence of such an integrated pipeline constitutes a research and
development gap that this project directly addresses through Health Intelligence, which
combines a rigorously benchmarked CNNversus-ViT classification module with a
geolocation-based hospital recommendation module and an AI-driven patient-support
chatbot.

Background of the Research Domain

frequently diagnosed cancer among men and the second most common among women,

variability, motivating the development of automated, deep learning-based diagnostic
support tools.

A growing body of work has applied CNN transfer learning to OSCC histopathology
classification. Kumar et al. compared four CNN architectures — ResNet50, DenseNet201,
EfficientNetB0, and ConvNeXt_Tiny — on a 10,000-image OSCC histopathology dataset,
reporting that EfficientNetB0 achieved the strongest overall performance, with
ConvNeXt_Tiny (a CNN architecture that borrows several transformer-inspired design
principles) placing a close second [4]. Similarly, Akram et al. proposed a Deep Visual
Detection System that benchmarked EfficientNetB3, DenseNet121, and ResNet50 across
two public datasets, Kaggle and NDB-UFES, finding that EfficientNetB3 consistently
outperformed the other two CNNs on both binary and multi-class OSCC classification
tasks [5].
More recent work has begun to directly incorporate Vision Transformers. Jagathesh and
Narayanan proposed a Convolutional Block Attention-aided Transformer Network that
fuses a ResNet50 backbone with a ViT encoder augmented by a convolutional attention
module, arguing that the hybrid design captures both local texture and global tissue
context better than either architecture alone [6]. Ramya and Minu introduced a
transformer-encoderassisted dilated convolution framework with global attention for
OSCC grading classification, motivated by the observation that most prior CNN-only
pipelines struggle to generalize across grading categories [7]. Li proposed a hybrid model
that fuses CrossAttention Vision Transformer (CrossViT) features with manually
engineered histopathological features, reporting accuracy above 99% on two
independent OSCC datasets [8].

Limitations of Existing Research
Across this body of work, three limitations recur. First, comparisons between CNN and ViT
architectures are typically embedded within the design of a single hybrid model rather
than reported as a systematic, side-by-side benchmark using a shared dataset,
preprocessing pipeline, and evaluation protocol, which makes it difficult to draw general
conclusions about the relative merits of the two paradigms for OSCC specifically. Second,
almost all reviewed studies restrict their contribution to the classification model itself and
stop short of any downstream patient-facing functionality, such as care navigation or
post-screening support. Third, none of the reviewed studies are contextualized for
Pakistan, despite the country's disproportionately high OSCC burden and limited access to
specialist oncology facilities outside major cities. Health Intelligence is designed to
directly address these three limitations by (i) benchmarking CNN and ViT architectures
under an identical protocol on OSCC histopathology data, and (ii) extending the resulting
classification model into a Pakistan-specific, location-aware hospital recommendation and
patient-support system.


4. Project Rationale
Oral cancer is a largely preventable but frequently late-diagnosed disease in Pakistan,
where the shortage of trained oral pathologists and specialist oncology centers means
that many patients only discover their condition once it has progressed to an advanced,
harder-to-treat stage. The motivation for this project is to reduce that diagnostic delay by
pairing an accurate, well-benchmarked AI classification model with practical,
location-aware guidance that helps a patient act on a screening result rather than simply
receiving it. Through this project, the team hopes to gain hands-on experience in
comparative deep learning research (specifically CNN versus ViT benchmarking on
medical images), in building and integrating a full-stack, AI-powered web application, and
in designing patient-centered health-support features responsibly and empathetically.


a. Aims and Objectives
    •​ To develop and preprocess a high-quality, unified OSCC histopathology image
       dataset drawn from public repositories for model training and evaluation.
    •​ To design, train, and evaluate CNN-based (ResNet/EfficientNet/DenseNet) and
       Vision Transformer-based models for binary OSCC classification, comparing their
       performance using accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and AUC-ROC.
    •​ To build a geolocation-based hospital recommendation module that maps a
       patient's location to the nearest oncology/ENT facilities from a curated Pakistani
       hospital database.
    •​ To design an AI-driven chatbot that supports patients with medication reminders,
       dietary and lifestyle precautions, and moral support.
    •​ To integrate all three modules into a single, usable web application, and to
       evaluate the system's overall usability and diagnostic reliability.

b. Scope of the Project
The scope of FYP-I covers problem formulation, dataset acquisition and preprocessing,

5. Proposed Methodology and Architecture
The proposed system, Health Intelligence, consists of three interconnected modules: (a) a
histopathology image classification pipeline comparing CNN and ViT architectures, (b) a
geolocation-based hospital recommendation engine, and (c) an AI-powered
patient-support chatbot, all accessed through a single web application front end. a.
Classification a. Pipeline
The classification pipeline follows the standard stages: Data Collection → Data
Preprocessing → Feature Extraction → Model Development → Training → Validation →
Testing → Evaluation → Interpretation. H&E-stained OSCC histopathology images will be
collected from public repositories (Section 9). Preprocessing will include patch
extraction/resizing, normalization, and data augmentation (flipping, rotation) to address
class imbalance. Two families of models will then be trained on identical data splits: (i)
CNN architectures — ResNet50, EfficientNet, and DenseNet — fine-tuned via transfer
learning from ImageNet weights, and (ii) a Vision Transformer (ViT) fine-tuned on the
same patches. Both families will be trained with the same optimizer settings, batch sizes,
and early-stopping criteria to ensure a fair comparison. Model performance will be
evaluated and compared using accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and AUC-ROC, along
with confusion-matrix analysis.

b. Hospital Recommendation Module
Once an image is classified, the pipeline below determines the recommended next step
for the patient:

     Fig. 1. High-level system flow from histopathology image to hospital recommendation.

The pipeline in Fig. 1 begins with the histopathology image being passed to the CNN/ViT
classification model. If SCC is detected, the system flags the case as "medical review
recommended" and proceeds to request the patient's location. This location is matched
against a curated database of oncology and ENT hospitals across Pakistan, and a
distancecalculation algorithm (using the haversine formula over hospital
latitude/longitude coordinates, refined with a mapping API for road-network distance
where available) ranks and returns the nearest suitable facilities, along with their name,
distance, and contact details, e.g., "Hospital 1 — 3.2 km", "Hospital 2 — 5.7 km",
"Hospital 3 — 8.1 km". Planned extensions to this module include filtering by facility type
(oncology center, ENT clinic, general hospital with oncology ward), display of estimated
travel time, and integration of user reviews/ratings where available.


c. AI Patient-Support Chatbot
The chatbot module is designed to support patients after a positive or suspicious
screening result. It will provide (i) medication reminders scheduled around a
patient-entered treatment plan, (ii) precautionary guidance on diet and lifestyle choices
relevant to oral cancer patients (e.g., avoiding tobacco, betel quid, and irritant foods), and
(iii) general moral support through empathetic, non-diagnostic conversational responses.
The chatbot will be built on a large language model accessed via API, with careful prompt
design and guardrails to ensure it does not attempt to replace clinical advice, and that it
consistently directs the patient toward the recommended hospitals and their treating
physician for any decision requiring medical judgment.

d. Web Application Architecture
The complete system will be delivered as a web application with a React.js-based front
end, a REST API back end (Node.js/FastAPI), a relational/NoSQL database for the hospital
directory and user data, and a separate model-serving component that hosts the trained


                                             10
CNN and ViT classifiers for inference. The three modules described above communicate
through this back end, allowing a single user session to move from image upload, to
classification result, to hospital recommendation, to ongoing chatbot support.

9. Expected Results
This project is expected to produce: (i) a comparative performance table quantifying the
accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and AUC-ROC of CNN-based versus ViT-based models
on OSCC histopathology classification, together with an analysis of the trade-offs
between the two paradigms in terms of accuracy and computational cost; (ii) a
functioning geolocation-based hospital recommendation module capable of returning
ranked, distanceannotated oncology/ENT facilities for a given patient location within
Pakistan; (iii) a working AI chatbot prototype providing medication reminders,
dietary/lifestyle precautions, and supportive conversation for OSCC patients; and (iv) a
fully integrated web application demonstrating the end-to-end flow from histopathology
image upload to classification result, hospital recommendation, and ongoing chatbot
support. Collectively, these deliverables are expected to demonstrate that combining a
rigorously benchmarked detection model with practical, location-aware patient support is
both technically feasible and of direct relevance to reducing diagnostic and
care-navigation delay for OSCC patients in Pakistan.
